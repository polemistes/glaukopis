# 0063 — The list is the tree, the path says where you are, and the window opens as it was left

Date: 2026-10-23. Status: proposed by the owner on 2026-10-23, the day after 0062 was built, and accepted by them in outline in the same message; adjusted by the owner the same day, after the assessment: the top row holds the paths and Split view alone, the rest stays on the bars under it for now, and the second project stays a guest for now, both projects to be of the same status later; the other choices under *Choices made in writing this* are the assessor's and stand until the owner says otherwise; to be built from `docs/plans/the-list-the-path-and-the-window-as-it-was-left.md`. Supersedes 0050 (the owl map behaves as a tree) and 0051 (the map of the projects as one map). Amends 0031 (folders of projects: the map goes, the list stays), 0033 (the pane of projects: on by default, the one tree), 0037 (the owl map, and side by side) and 0062 (the home, the fourth place of the keys, the menus of a project and a folder, Side by side).

## Context

The owner, after using what 0062 built the day before: the last reworking of the interface does not work; it must be simplified and clarified a lot. Their proposal: give up the mind map from the owl down to the inmost element, losing the owl map and its diagram; the pane at the left, with the tree of projects, with folders, and with the whole tree shown under a project, is the thing. When Glaukopis opens it should look as much as possible as it did when it was closed: the same projects open, the same maps open, the text where it was in the text, the diagram where it was. Open projects can be closed. With no project open, the screen shows the owl, a button for a new project and the projects last opened. *Side by side* is renamed *Split view*. It must be much easier to see whether the two views are of one project or of two. The toolbars are to be redesigned, and first the top bar: it holds the full path of the open element with a dropdown icon that opens a hierarchical menu of all projects; when the view is split, the split goes all the way up to the top bar, each side with its path; a path that does not fit is elided so that the topmost and the bottommost levels still show; the active view and the other differ in colour with quite high contrast; the views can switch sides. After this, the complete rearrangement of the toolbars is to be planned.

What the assessment found, from the code and the records:

- **The map promised one tree and could not keep the promise.** It drew folders, projects and elements as one picture, but a project is a document with rules of its own: it cannot go into a project, its elements are read from disk, its panels, its sharing and its history are not the map's. So the rules changed at the project without the picture saying so; 0062 saw this and drew regions around folders, which did not mend it. Every rule added to the map was a rule the list at the left already kept without a picture.
- **The list at the left already does what is wanted.** It shows the folders, the projects in them, the tree of the open project down to the element in view, and the tree of a closed project read from disk (0037, 0062); it has the keys of the tree, the one menus, dragging, and undo through the message. What it lacks is to be the only place; to be on by default (it is off, `projectsPane: false`); to mark both projects of two sides; and a few things that lived in the map's bar: the ways to begin, the deleted projects, the project of all the projects.
- **What is kept today, and what is not.** The view of a project is kept with the project (`view-store.svelte.ts`, `layout.ts`): its panes with their element and view, the cameras of its diagrams, the panel at the side, the outline, the preview, the sizes and what is folded in the text; a second side showing another project is kept and comes back. So a project opened again looks as it was left, but for the place in the text, which is not kept, and the side that had the focus. What is not kept at all is which project was open: the window opens at the home every time, since the address is empty at start. The window's size and place are not kept either.
- **Two sides are hard to tell apart.** The bar of the pane with the focus is tinted a shade off paper; the name of the project stands in a pane's bar only where the two panes show two projects, and small; the bar of the project over both panes (Diagram/Text, Side by side, Preview, Share, undo, redo, the panel) says nothing of which side it acts on.

The proposal is the shape every program with documents and folders has: a tree at the left, documents opened and closed, a path over each document. It is the least likely to need a fourth reworking.

After the assessment, the owner: the real decisions about the tools of the window come afterwards; the top bar holds only the path of the current element with its dropdown, and perhaps the Split view button; the rest goes on a bar under it for now, and the owner will think hard about the rearrangement after this is built. The second project may stay a guest for now, if that makes the changes simpler, but in the end both projects are of the same status; whether each needs its own history, and whether both may be shared, are questions for the next phase.

## Decision

### The list at the left is the tree

- The list at the left (the pane of projects of 0033) is the one place where the folders, the projects and the elements are shown as a tree: the folders as rows that open and close, the projects in them, under an open project its tree down to the element in view, under a closed project its tree read from disk when it is unfolded. It is on by default, and stays as it was left. It keeps the keys of the tree of 0062, which has three places now, the diagram, the text and the list: the arrows; Tab and Shift+Tab; Alt+Enter for a new project and Alt+Shift+Enter for a new folder; Alt with the arrows to move into a folder or out, and an element of the open project as in its map; Alt+Shift+Down to open or enter; Enter as a click; F2; Delete; Shift+F10. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z in the list undo and redo the changes to folders and projects, as they did on the map.
- **Its head**: *Projects*; a plus whose menu has *New project…*, *New folder*, then *Join a shared project…*, *A project from a document…*, *Open a project file…*, *Open an Org file…*; a ⋯ whose menu has *Deleted projects…*, with their count, and *A project of all the projects…*; and ×, which hides the list. Under the head, the field that finds a project by its name and the button for a new folder, as today. The list has one form, the folders: the *Last used* form goes, since the start screen shows the projects last opened.
- **The owl row** at the top of the list goes with the map; the list begins with the folders and the projects in none.
- **A project is opened and closed; an element is entered and left.** The menu of a project: *Open*, disabled with *It is open now* for one that is shown; *Open beside*, while another project is open and this is not shown; *Close*, for one that is shown; *Unfold* or *Fold*, where its tree is shown under it; then *Rename*, *Duplicate*, *History…*, *Save as a file…*, *Save as Org…*; then *Move to folder ▸*, *Out of folders*; then *Delete*. The menu of a folder: *Unfold* or *Fold*; *New project here*, *New folder here*, *Rename*; *Move to folder ▸*, *Out of folders*; *Delete the folder*. A folder is not entered anywhere now, so *Enter* leaves its menu. The menu of an element under a project in the list keeps *Enter*, *Enter beside* and *Unfold*/*Fold* (0062). Alt+Shift+Down on a project's row opens it and on an element's row enters it; Enter does what a click does.
- **The list says what is shown.** The project of each side is marked in the list, with its tree under it down to the element in view of that side; the project of the side that has the focus is marked strongly, the other quietly.
- **Alt+Shift+Up at the top of a project** goes on up into the list: the list is shown if it was hidden, and the project's row takes the focus, so that the keys of the tree go on from there.

### Nothing open: the start screen

- `#/` is the start screen, shown while no project is open, beside the list: the owl; *New project*; and the projects last opened, newest first, up to ten, each with its name and its badge, the folder it lies in and when it was last changed, a click opening it. With no project at all it is the welcome of today: *Welcome to Glaukopis*, the line under it, and the buttons that begin a project, join one, make one from a document, open a project file and open an Org file. A document dropped on the start screen becomes a project of its own, as one dropped on the map did.
- The projects last opened are a list of ids in the settings, written whenever a project is opened; one that no longer exists is left out when the list is shown.
- The house in the rail and Ctrl+1 go to the projects: to the project shown last in this run, at its element and in its view, where there was one; else to the start screen.

### The window opens as it was left

- The settings keep **the place** the window was at, as the address writes it, a moment after every change and before the window closes. At start, when the address names nothing, the place kept is gone to: a project at its element and in its view, with its second side as the project's view keeps it; the library, the pictures, the files or the search as they were. A project that is gone gives the start screen.
- What the view of a project keeps already stays as it is, and gains three things: **the element at the top of the text** of each side that shows the text, which the text scrolls to when the side is laid out again; **which side had the focus**; and **whether the sides are switched**. The diagram's camera, kept per element already, brings the diagram back where it was.
- **The window's size and place**, and whether it was maximised, are kept in the settings when the window closes and brought back before it is shown; the window is made hidden and shown once the settings are read, which also takes away the white flash at start.
- Nothing is kept that was not kept before: what is written is in the project; a dialog that was open is not opened again.

### Two sides: Split view

- *Side by side* is called **Split view**. Its button opens its menu: *This element, as diagram and text*; *Another element…*; *Another project…*; and, while two sides are shown, *Switch sides* and *One at a time*, which closes the side without the focus. Ctrl+Shift+S opens the menu. The button is lit while two sides are shown.
- **Each side's bar has ×**, which closes that side; with one side it closes the project, and the start screen comes. **Ctrl+W** closes the side that has the focus, the same way. *Close* in the list's menu of a project closes the sides that show it.
- **The active side** is the one that has the focus: the keys, undo and redo act in its project; the preview and Look follow it, as today. Its cell of the top bar is in the accent colour, with the accent's ink; the other side's cell is sunken and grey. The bodies of the sides are not tinted, so that the text reads as it did. With one side, nothing is coloured.
- **One project or two** is read from the paths: each begins with the project's name, in bold with its badge, so two names are two projects, and one name twice is one; the line between two projects stays doubled, as 0037 has it.
- **Of two projects, the one opened first is still the project of the view**: the panel at the side (the references, the pictures, the comments, the history, the changes, the loose citations), the sharing, the presence and the bar of words are its; the second is written in, moved about and undone as the first, and shown as diagram or text. While the second side has the focus, the head of the panel at the side names the project it is about. Closing the first's side makes the second the project of the view, opened anew at the element it was at. The owner has agreed to this for now and decided that in the end the two projects are of the same status; the record of the rearrangement of the toolbars says how. Each project has its own history on disk and its own connection to the others already, the guest included; what the second lacks is the panels that show them, and the view built around one project.

### The top bar: the path

- **One row** at the top of the work, split as the sides are: a cell for each side, as wide as the side, the line between the cells over the line between the sides; in each cell the path, with its chevron and its ⋯, and nothing else; at the right end of the row, outside the cells, *Split view* alone, since it is the button that makes the sides. **Under it, the bar of the project** as it is today, less *Side by side*: the view chooser of the active side (*Diagram*, *Text*, and *Timeline* where there is one), *Preview*, *Share* with the presence, *Not saved*, undo, redo and the panel at the side. **The bar of each side** stays as it is today, less where-you-are, which the path has: the button of the list in the first, the outline, the writing tools while the side shows the text, the view chooser of the side while two are shown, and ×, which closes the side, shown always now that one side can be closed alone. The real decisions about these tools come with the rearrangement of the toolbars, which the owner will think through after this is built; nothing in the two lower bars is meant to last.
- **The path** fills a cell. First a chevron, which opens the **menu of all projects**: the folders as submenus, the projects as items, in the order of the list, the projects shown marked; a project chosen is shown in this side, at its top. Then the crumbs, from the top down: the folders the project lies in, small; the project, in bold with its badge; the elements from the top of the project down to the element in view, the last in the strongest type. Each crumb is a button: a folder opens the menu of all projects at that folder; the project shows the project at its top; an element enters it. After the last crumb, ⋯ *This element*, with what can be done with the element in view, as the bar has it today: *Rename*, *What this document is…*, *About this document…*, *Loose citations…*, *Languages…*, *New element under it*, *A document into it…*, *Delete*. The pull-down of the elements beside the one in view goes: the list has them.
- **Elision.** Where the path does not fit its cell, the crumbs in the middle hide behind one crumb, *…*, so that the first crumb and the last always show, and then as many of the rest as fit, taken from the end; *…* opens a menu of the hidden crumbs, each doing what the crumb does. A name too long for the room is cut with an ellipsis, the last crumb's last. The path is measured again when the cell changes width.

### What goes

The map of all projects and all that was only for it: its view and its diagram, its keys and its place in the sheet of keys, the places by hand and *Place everything by itself*, *Show all of it*, the regions, the maps of projects unfolded on it, the route `#/owl`, the owl row of the list, *All projects* and the owl at the left of the bar of a project, the word *map* for it; the *Last used* form of the list; the pull-down of siblings in the bar; *Enter* for a folder. Dragging a folder or a project between the list and the map goes with the map; dragging among the rows of the list stays. The core keeps its commands for the map's places and the list's form, which nothing calls; a later cleaning takes them out.

## Choices made in writing this, which the owner has not been asked about

1. **A project is opened and closed, an element is entered and left.** 0062 made *Enter* the one word for going in, opening a project included, because the map, the list and the diagram said it three ways. With the map gone and *Close* wanted, *Open* and *Close* are the pair every program has, and a project coming into the window is not the act of the view narrowing to an element. The keys do not change.
2. **The dropdown stands at the start of the path**, not at its end, so that the path reads *choose ▾ › where you are* and two menus (▾ and ⋯) do not stand side by side at the end.
3. **Split view stands at the right end of the top row**, the one tool there besides the paths, since it makes the sides; the owner left it open. Everything else stays in the bar of the project under it and in the bars of the sides, moved as little as possible, until the rearrangement of the toolbars.
4. **The active side is coloured in its bar only**, in the accent with the accent's ink, the other sunken and grey: the eye finds it at once, and the text and the diagram keep their paper.
5. **Switching sides mirrors them; it does not change which project is the project of the view.** The first-opened project keeps the panels, the preview's home, the sharing and the history. Making the sides equal means building the view of a project anew around two projects (its store, its pictures, its panels, its review); the owner has agreed to the guest for now, on the understanding that both are of the same status in the end, decided with the rearrangement of the toolbars.
6. **Ctrl+W closes the side with the focus**, since it closes a document in every program; *One at a time* closes the side without the focus, as today.
7. **The *Last used* form of the list goes**: one list with one form, since the start screen shows the last opened, and the field finds a project by its name.
8. **The projects last opened are kept in the settings**, as a list of ids, not in the core's arrangement: they are a thing of this window, and the settings are written before the window closes.
9. **The place kept is the address**, so that the library, the pictures, the files and the search come back as well as a project, with one setting.
10. **The text keeps the element at its top**, not a pixel: a pixel does not hold across window sizes, fonts and edits; the element at the top is what the eye remembers, and the outline knows it already.
11. **Alt+Shift+Up at the top of a project goes into the list**, since the tree goes on there: the project's row takes the focus.
12. **The window's size and place are kept by the application itself, in the settings**, not through Tauri's window-state plugin: the plugin writes a file of its own beside the configuration, outside the data folder the tests and the owner's rules keep apart, and the settings are flushed before the window closes anyway.
13. **Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z in the list** undo and redo changes to folders and projects, as the map had them, so that the undo of the home keeps a key.
14. **The core keeps the map's commands**, since taking them out changes the generated contract for nothing the owner sees; a cleaning later.
15. **The button says *New project***, in the voice of the rest, where the owner wrote *Create new project*.

## Why not otherwise

- *Keep the map as a view one can visit, beside the list*: two pictures of one hierarchy again, with two menus, which 0062 set out to mend and could not.
- *Tabs of open projects, as editors have*: a third notion beside the list and the sides. Here nothing is open that is not shown: the list marks what the sides show.
- *Make the two sides equal now*: see choice 5.
- *Keep the pixel of the text's scroll*: see choice 10.
- *Remember which project was open, and nothing else*: cheaper, but the owner asked for the window as it was left, and one setting of the address does it for every place.

## Consequences

- `OwlView.svelte`, `OwlMap.svelte`, `owl.ts`, `owl-keys.ts` and their tests go; `StartView.svelte`, `Path.svelte`, `path.ts` and `projects-menu.ts` come; `MapBar.svelte` goes into `Path.svelte`; the route `owl` becomes `home`; `listUi` loses the map's folds, reveal and maps; `projects.places`, `projects.shown` and their calls go.
- The settings gain `lastPlace`, `recentProjects` and `window`; `projectsPane` is true where nothing was chosen; the view of a project gains `tops`, `focused` and `mirrored`.
- The table of keys loses the place `owl`; gains `close-side` (Ctrl+W) and `switch-sides` (no key); `side-by-side` is called Split view; `tree` holds in three places; the list's undo and redo are entries of their own, over the project's.
- The words of the map go from `home.ftl` in both languages; the new words are written in English and Norwegian Bokmål together; the other languages lag, as usual.
- `docs/guide.md`: *Many projects* and *The map of the projects* become *The list of projects* and *The start screen*; *One tree, entered anywhere* says the path and Split view; *Keys* loses the map; a short section says what comes back at start.
- `e2e/owl.mjs` becomes `e2e/list.mjs`; `projects.mjs`, `tree-keys.mjs`, `housekeeping.mjs`, `maps.mjs`, `languages.mjs`, `new-project-view.mjs`, `keys.mjs` and `look.mjs` are revised where they used the map or *Side by side*; `e2e/as-left.mjs` and `e2e/path.mjs` are new.
