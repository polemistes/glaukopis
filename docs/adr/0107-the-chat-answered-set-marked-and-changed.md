# 0107 — The chat answered, set, marked and changed

Date: 2026-11-16. Status: built on 2026-11-16, its choices not yet confirmed by the owner; from the owner's words of the same day ("In the shared project chat, it would be nice with some adjustments: Answering specific messages, which then are placed as answers to them. Formatting in the chat text, at least bold, italics etc. Emojis. A mark telling who has read the message. Possibility to edit the text."), with two choices the owner made when asked: that an answer stands under the message it answers, however old that message is, and not at the foot with the message quoted; and that emojis are both in the text and reactions to a message. Amends 0092 (a message can be changed; who has read is kept in the project too) and 0094 (what is kept of the chat's field holds its marks and what it answers or changes). The plan: `docs/plans/the-chat-answered-set-marked-and-changed-and-a-paragraph-made-an-element.md`, part B, with the choosing of emojis as part C.

## Context

- **The chat of a shared project** (0092) was a list of plain messages in the project's own document, each with who wrote it, when and its words; outside undo, shared, saved and kept in the history with no machinery of its own. A message could be deleted by its writer and never changed. Its field was a plain text area, and what was written in it and not sent was kept with the project's view on this computer (0094).
- **What was read was known on this computer alone**: the message read last, kept with the view, counted the unread on the tab and the dot on the Document button. Nobody else could see that one had read.
- **The comments** (0024) already answer, under a thread, and change a note of one's own, with *changed* after it: the chat could say the same words.
- **The text's editor** is ProseMirror, with marks for bold, italics, underlined, struck through and code, each drawn and keyed in one place (`editor/schema.ts`, the plugins). The undo of a project is Yjs's, over the project's document.

## Decision

1. **A message gains three things, each left out where it has none**: `to`, the message it answers; `marks`, how stretches of its words are set (from a sign to another, of five kinds); and `edited`, when its writer last changed it. A message of before is read as it was, and an earlier version of the program shows a later message as its plain words. What another copy wrote is read with care: a mark of a kind not known, or outside the words, is dropped, not the message.
2. **Answers are one deep**: an answer to an answer answers what that one answered, and an answer to a message that is not there answers none.
3. **One's own message can be changed**, and nobody else's: it is put in again where it stands, its id, its writer, when it was written and what it answers kept, and when it was changed set. Where it was changed on two computers at once, the one changed last is read.
4. **Who has read what is kept in the project**, in a map of its own, `chatRead`: for each person their name, the message they read last and when. Each window writes its own entry while the chat is shown with the window in front and the project is shared, where it differs. The mark on this computer still counts the unread.
5. **Reactions are kept in a map of their own**, `chatReactions`, one entry for each person's one emoji on one message, so that two who react at once both stay. Anyone reacts to any message, with as many different emojis as they like; a second press takes one's own back. What is not an emoji is not read as one.
6. **All of it is outside undo**, with the origin of the chat, and none of it is in the scope of the undo manager. Deleting the conversation empties all three. A message taken back keeps its reactions, so that *Undo* brings them back with it.
7. **The history makes no session of any of it**: a record all of whose changes are in the chat, in who has read, in the reactions, or among who deleted what, is left out, as a message was. A mark of who has read is written each time someone reads, and would else be a session each time.
8. **An answer stands under the message it answers**, after the answers already there, in the order they were written, set in with a thin line at its left. The messages that answer none stand as before, each with its answers under it: nothing ever moves. Under one name stand messages of one person within five minutes on one day, as before, among the messages that answer none (a message with answers ends its group) and among the answers to one message. An answer written on another day than the day line it stands under shows its date with its time.
9. **Answers whose message was taken back stay together**, where the first of them stood, under a quiet line, *A message that was deleted*; *Undo* of the deleting puts the message back over them.
10. **Answering**: *Answer* on a message puts a line over the field, *Answer to* its writer, with its first words and a ×; the cursor goes to the field. Pressed on an answer, the line names that answer and its writer, and what is sent answers the same message. An answer sent is brought into view. Escape gives the answering up and keeps what is written.
11. **The field is a small editor of ProseMirror of its own**, not bound to the project: lines with the marks bold, italic, underlined, struck through and code, whose specs it shares with the text's. Enter sends, Shift+Enter is a new line, the marks come by the keys the text has for them, and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y are an undo of the field's own, which never reaches the project's. Pasting keeps the line breaks and the five marks of what is pasted, and the rest comes as plain words. Under the field, one row: the buttons of the five marks, the button of the emojis, which puts each one chosen where the cursor is, and *Send*.
12. **A message is drawn from its words and marks**, never as HTML. An address of the web is a link, opened in the system's browser as links are elsewhere; a message of one to three emojis alone is shown larger; its words are set in the font of the interface with the fonts of emojis after it.
13. **What is written and not sent is kept as before** (0094), in the same field of the drafts, now as JSON with its marks and what it answers or changes. Plain words that an earlier version kept are read as words. Where nothing is written and nothing answered or changed, the field is dropped from the drafts.
14. **Changing**: *Change* in the menu of one's own message, while the project is shared, takes its words and marks into the field under the line *Changing a message*. Enter keeps the change, Escape gives it up, and what was being written before comes back. A message changed says *changed* after its words, with when in its tooltip; it is not unread again, and nothing is told of it.
15. **Who has read** stands at the end of each message, at its last line: the small circles of those who have read it, but its writer and oneself, three at most and then `+n`, with *Read by* and their names in the tooltip. Someone has read a message when the message they read last is that one or one after it, or, where that message is gone, by when it was written.
16. **Reactions** stand under the words as chips, an emoji and how many gave it, one's own in the accent; the tooltip names who; pressing a chip gives or takes back one's own. Nothing is told of a reaction.
17. **The tools of a message** come over its top right corner when the pointer is on it or the focus within it: *React*, *Answer*, and on one's own the three dots, with *Change* and *Delete*. The right-click menu has *Answer*, *React…*, and on one's own *Change* and *Delete*. While the project is not shared nothing can be written: no *Answer*, *React* or *Change*, and a chip cannot be pressed; one's own message can still be deleted.
18. **What is new is marked and led to**: the messages of others unread when the chat came to be shown, and those that arrive while it is shown, are new until the chat is not shown or one sends a message oneself. A new message has a thin bar of the accent at its left, until it has been within what is shown and others have arrived after it: the bar is for the latest news and for what has not been seen, not for everything that came while the chat was shown. When the chat comes to be shown, the first new message is brought near the top, and with none the list is at its foot, as before. While new messages have not been within what is shown of the list, a button over its foot says how many, with an arrow up or down, and goes to the nearest of them. The list still stays at its foot when a message arrives there while it is at its foot.

19. **Every tab of the document pane stays within the pane.** A shared project has eight tabs there since the notes of the notebook became one (0101), and at the pane's usual width the last of them, the chat, stood out past the × and could not be pressed. The tab chosen says its name while there is room for it; where the tabs with a name among them do not fit, the name is left out, and the tab is an icon with its name in its tooltip, as the others are (`project/SideTabs.svelte`, which measures its own room).

## Choices made in building this, which the owner has not been asked about

1. **Answers one deep** (2); **the answers of a message taken back kept together under a line** (9); **the answer written in the field at the foot** under a line that names what is answered, not in a field under the message (10).
2. **The five marks, their buttons, and no signs typed for them** (11): nothing like `*bold*` is read as a mark. **Code has no key**, as it has none in the text.
3. **Links and larger emojis** (12). An address runs to the next space, and a full stop, a comma, a closing bracket or quotation mark at its end is not taken as part of it.
4. **A change written in the field at the foot; *changed* after the words** (14). *Send* keeps a change as Enter does; the button keeps its word.
5. **Who has read on every message as circles, without oneself and the writer**, kept in the project so that everyone who shares it sees when one has read; it cannot be turned off (4, 15).
6. **Reactions by anyone on any message, as many as one likes, told to nobody** (5, 16). An emoji is told from other things as a short string, without spaces, that holds a pictograph or a keycap.
7. **How what is new is marked and led to** (18). Where new messages not seen stand both above and below, the button points to the nearest.
8. **Answer pressed while a message is being changed gives the change up first**, and what was being written before comes back with the answering.
9. **An answering whose message is gone becomes a message that answers none, and a changing whose message is gone is given up**, when the panel sees it; what was kept in the drafts is mended then, not when the view is saved.
10. **A message changed on two computers at once** keeps the place of the first copy and the words of the one changed last.
11. **The field's undo** makes a step of what is written without a pause of more than 0.6 seconds, keeps a hundred steps, and begins anew when what stands in the field is set from outside (a message taken to be changed, what was written before coming back).
12. **The choosing of emojis** (part C of the plan), a widget of its own, `ui/EmojiPicker.svelte`: a popover with a tab for each of Unicode's eight groups (Smileys, People, Animals and nature, Food and drink, Travel and places, Activities, Objects, Symbols), and before them Recently used while there are any. The list is made by `scripts/make-emoji-list.py` from Unicode's own `emoji-test.txt` and not typed: the 1,588 fully qualified emojis of Emoji 14.0 and earlier, so that the fonts of the systems people have hold them, without skin tones and without the flags of countries, which Windows draws none of; the few other flags stand last among the symbols. No finding by name, which would need the names of every emoji in every language of the interface. The twenty-four chosen last are kept in the settings of this computer. Chosen several at a time, as into the field, the picker does not shuffle under the pointer: the new order shows when it is next opened. The arrows move through the grid and among the tabs, and closed, the focus goes back to where it was.
13. **The bar of a new message goes once it has been seen and others have arrived after it** (18), rather than staying on everything that came while the chat was shown: in a conversation watched as it is written, the bars would else run down the whole side of the list.
14. **The tab chosen gives up its name where the tabs do not fit** (19), rather than the tabs standing in two rows or scrolling sideways.
15. **A font of emojis is named by the packages, not brought along**: optional with the Arch packages, as the fonts of other scripts are, and recommended with the deb and the rpm; Windows and macOS have their own. Without one on Linux, an emoji is shown as the system can, in one colour or as an empty box.

## Not done

- No answers to answers shown as such; no naming of a person in a message; no spelling in the field; nothing told of a reaction or a change; a message changed is not unread again.
- The unread are still counted by the mark of this computer alone: read on one computer, a message is unread on another of the same person until it is shown there.
- An earlier version of the program shows an answer at the foot as a plain message, its marks as plain words, and neither who has read nor the reactions.

## Added to, 2026-11-16 (later)

The owner's words, of later the same day ("Let it be an option in settings to turn off notification of having read comments. […] In comments I forgot to ask for one thing. If I mark some of the text in a message or a reply and then reply to it or another message, or add a new message, the marked part of the text will show as what I am commenting on at the top of my new message, and pressing it takes me to the message/reply where it stands."); the *comments* are the messages of the chat. The plan: part E of its second round. What is above holds, but for choice 5, *who has read … cannot be turned off*, which is no longer true: it can (21–24).

### Decision

20. **A message can carry a quote**: `quote`, the id of the message the words are from and the words themselves, plain, trimmed, at most 500 characters and cut with an ellipsis where longer. The words are kept in the message that quotes, so that they stay as they were quoted when the message they are from is changed or taken back. A quote that is no object or has no words is left out when what another copy wrote is read; an earlier version of the program shows the message without it. `say` takes it as a third argument after what is answered, and a change of one's own message keeps it, as it keeps what the message answers.
21. **What is marked is caught at the press**, before the press itself changes what is selected: at `pointerdown`, in its capture phase, anywhere in the panel, the selection is read, and where it is not empty and both its ends are within the words of one message of the chat, an answer too, the words selected are remembered for that press with the message's id. A selection over more than one message, or anywhere else (the quote at the top of a message among them), is nothing. A right click selects the word under it on macOS, and a press in the field takes the selection away: this is why it is read then.
22. **What was caught is used by what the press begins**: *Answer* among the tools of a message; *Answer* in the right-click menu, whose entry holds what was caught when the menu was opened; and a press within the field, which begins a new message or goes on with what is written. *Answer* on the message the words are from answers it with the quote; on another message it answers that one, with the quote of the first. Words caught while a quote stands over the field take its place; a press with nothing marked changes nothing. While a message is being changed, no quote is caught by a press in the field.
23. **Over the field, a line of its own for the quote**, above the line of what is answered where both are there: the quotation mark, the words on one line, cut with an ellipsis where they do not fit, and a ×. **Escape** gives up a change, as before; else it gives up the answering and the quote, both where both are there, and keeps what is written. What is written keeps its quote in the drafts (0094), and a quote with no words written keeps the field there; nothing is sent without words.
24. **Sent, the quote stands at the top of the message**, over its words, quiet, with a line at its left, three lines at most. Pressed, it brings the message it is from into the middle of the list and lights it for a moment, a background that fades within a second and a half; its tooltip names who wrote that message. Where that message is gone, the quote stays as words and cannot be pressed.
25. **A setting, *Show the others what I have read in the chat***, under *Your details* in the settings, on unless turned off, kept by the interface alone. Off, one's mark of how far one has read is written into no project, and where a project holds one's mark from before, it is taken out when its chat is next shown with the window in front while the project is shared, so that one's circle goes from the others' messages. On again, the mark is written at the next such showing. One still sees the circles of the others. The mark on this computer, which counts the unread, is untouched by the setting, and the history makes no session of a mark taken out, as of one written.

### Choices made in building this, which the owner has not been asked about

16. **The quote is caught at any press in the field**, so words marked only to be copied are quoted too, until the × or Escape: this is what the owner's words say.
17. **500 characters; three lines shown; the words kept in the quoting message** (20, 24).
18. **The setting's place and words; that turning it off also takes one's earlier marks out; that the others' circles are still shown to someone who shows none** (25).
19. ***Answer* with nothing marked keeps a quote already over the field**, as a press in the field does; ***Answer* pressed while a message is being changed** gives the change up, as before, and quotes what was marked, the press having ended the change.
20. **The × of each line gives up that line alone**: the × of the line of what is answered now leaves the quote, and the × of the quote leaves the answering; Escape gives up both.
21. **A key pressed in the panel forgets what was caught**, so that *Answer* reached by the keyboard quotes nothing marked at an earlier press.
22. **A quote whose id another copy wrote as something other than words** is kept as words that cannot be pressed; a quote too long is cut, not left out.
23. **The setting stands under *Your details***, as the plan said, drawn as *Tell me when a new version is out* is, with *On* and *Off* of its own.

### Not done

- A selection that runs from the words of a message into *changed* after them, or into the next message (a triple click can end there), quotes nothing, since both its ends must be within the words of one message.
- No quote of a quote: the quote at the top of a message cannot be marked to be quoted again.
