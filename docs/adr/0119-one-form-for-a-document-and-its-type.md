# 0119 — One form for a document and for its type; a document type is made from a document; "template" and "layout" are not said

Date: 2026-11-22. Status: built on 2026-11-22; holds. The second and third stages of `docs/plans/one-form-and-the-order-of-the-tree.md`. From the owner's words of that day (the last two sections of `docs/brief.md`): the word *template* confuses, a *layout* is no thing of its own to the writer and Look and Layout are mixed up, nothing should be "as the … has it" or "my own from this", there should be one place where every detail of how a document is made is decided, for one document and for a document type, and a writer must be able to take away anything a type has. Takes the place of the template editor of 0038 and 0042, of the part *Layout* of Look and the ways of keeping a layout of one's own in 0042 and 0065, and of the table that told template, layout and reference style apart in 0054.

## Context

- **Two places said how a document is made.** Look set the pages of one document, over its layout; the editor of a template, a window of its own, set what a type consisted of, brought, offered and was exported as. A writer who had set a document up as they wanted it had to go to the editor and say it all again, to make a type of it.
- **Three words for one thing.** A *template* was what the document is, a *layout* how its pages look, a *reference style* how it cites; the guide had a table to tell them apart, and Look had a part *Layout* beside the part for the type. The writer thinks of one thing: the kind of document they are writing, which has pages and parts.
- **Values were named by what they were not.** *As the template has it*, *Keep these changes as a layout of my own*, *take Manuscript's again*: a value said where it was not from.

## Decision

1. **Look is the form.** The writer sets a document up as they want it, in Look, seeing the pages, and then says where that goes. Underneath nothing changes: a template, a layout, a reference style, the document's changes over the layout (`DocumentSettings.look`) and over the template (`composition`).
2. **The words.** *Template* is *document type* (*dokumenttype*) in every message of `en` and `nb`, in the glossary and in the guide; *layout* is not said in Look, nor outside it where it meant how the pages look: Look says it, or the look. A value is named with where it is from: *Chicago, from Novel*, *Manuscript, your default*, *Manuscript, from The book*; a change is *Changed in this document* or *Changed in “The book”*, and what takes it away is **Reset**. A message whose meaning changed has a new name, so that the old translations are pruned and not shown wrong; the other languages are translated in a round.
3. **The top of Look** names the document type, with **Document type…**, which opens the chooser, and one line of state: *n changes in this document* (the changes of the look and of what is required, of the element and those over it), with **Save to “…”**, where the type is the writer's own, **Save as document type…** and **Reset**, which takes every change of the document away, in one step of undo.
4. **Saving to a type** is one function (`saveToType`, `look/to-type.ts`). It saves the look with all the changes as a layout of the writer's own (over the type's own, where it has one; else a new one named as the type, based on the look in use); it makes the template of the type in use with the document's changes of what is required laid over it, that layout, the reference style where the document or the type chose one, and, as its contents, the parts the document has (`typeContents`, pure and tested; the order they stand in the tree, the text where it begins; `document/stands.ts` is shared with the checklist); and it takes what the document set of the look, the style and what is required off the elements from the highest that set any of it, in one step of undo. With a name it makes a new type of the writer's own (`asOwn`) and gives it to the document.
5. **Editing a type.** `lookUi.editing` is the id of the type being edited. While it is the type of the document Look is on, Look says *Editing the document type “…”* with **Done**, and every change written is saved into the type a moment after (the same function), so the document carries none of its own. Done, closing Look, or another type for the document ends it.
6. **The last part of Look, Document type**, takes the place of *Layout* (`look/TypeSection.svelte`): **Look taken from**, which is the first choice named with where it is from and then the looks by kind; **Changed in this document**, the list of changes with where each was made and **Reset**, what is required among them; and, only for a type of the writer's own, each saved into the type as it is changed: **Name**, **Description**, **Paragraph and words kinds offered first**, **Exported as**, and **Delete this document type…**, which asks, and the elements that used it use the type it was made from. The key of a kind stays where it is, on the kind in Look.
7. **The chooser** has **New document type…** at its top: a **Name** and what to **Start from**, any type, the one in use first, or **Nothing**, the plain type; **Create** makes the type, gives it to the element, closes Document properties and opens Look on it, editing. A type of the writer's own in use has **Edit**, which does the same for it.
8. **Gone**: the editor of a template (`TemplateEditor.svelte`, `template-editor.svelte.ts`, its host in `ProjectDialogs.svelte`) with the messages only it used; from Look *Keep these changes as a layout of my own…*, *Put these changes into “…”*, *Delete this layout of mine…*, the description and the source of the layout, the line *Layout: X · n changes*, and the foot button *Document properties…*; from the guide the table of template, layout and reference style. The layouts of the writer's own that exist stay among the looks to choose from and in the settings; nothing deletes them.

9. **The parts a type suggests are edited in Parts** (the owner's word of 2026-11-22, after the first build). While a type of the writer's own is edited, *Parts* lists the parts it suggests and only those; beside each an **×** (*Take out of “…”*) takes it out of the type's contents and the kinds it offers, and **Another part…** puts a part into it rather than adding an element: a part of the front before the text, one of the back after it and before the notes and the bibliography, the bibliography last, a cover among the kinds (`look/type-parts.ts`, pure and tested). Each is saved to the type at once. **Add** still adds an element to the document. The order of what a type suggests is not changed here: it is only told, by the checklist.

## Consequences

- A writer makes a document type by setting a document up, not by filling in a form of its own; and takes anything a type has away by making a type without it, or by editing one of their own.
- A type of the writer's own is still, underneath, a template and a layout of its own, made with it and named as it is. Nothing in the files or the contract changed; no Rust changed.
- The word *layout* remains in the names of messages, in the code and in the settings' names for files; the writer sees it in none of them.
- A type of the writer's own that is edited does not have its parts changed by the look written into it (choices, 12); they are changed in *Parts* while it is edited (decision 9), and by **Save to** and **Save as document type…**, which take the parts the document has.

## Choices made in building this, which the owner has not been asked about

1. **The part of Look is keyed `document-type`**, not `type`, which the brief named: `type` is already the key of the part for the typeface. Its chip says *Document type*.
2. **Where a type made of a document is set.** On the highest of: the element that sets the type, and the highest element on the way to the document that sets a look, a layout, a reference style or a change of what is required; else on the document. Below it the type is taken off (`makersOf`).
3. **The count** is the paths changed of the look, and of what is required, on the element and those over it. **Save to** is dimmed where nothing is changed, also where a look or a reference style was chosen (that counts as something set otherwise), and **Reset** takes the changes of the look and of what is required off every element on the way; a look or a style chosen is not taken off by Reset.
4. **The kinds a type offers are kept** when a document is saved into it: only its contents follow the document, and the kinds of cover that stand in the document are added. So a part the writer took out of the document is still suggested by *Parts*, as a kind the type offers, after the save.
5. **The notes** stay in the contents before the bibliography where the type had them; where it had none, none is added. **The text** stands where it begins in the document; where the document says no place for it, it is put before the first part of the back, or last.
6. **A new type's look is named as the type.** A reference style is saved in a type only where the document or the type chose one; otherwise it is left to the look.
7. **What a document requires otherwise than its type** is listed under **Changed in this document** with the changes of the look, each with its own **Reset**.
8. **Save as document type…** asks a name with the type's name and *changed* in the field (*Gentium book, changed*), and makes the type at once.
9. **Editing ends** where the type of the document becomes another, and where Look unmounts.
10. **The layout of a type of the writer's own carries the reference style of the layout it was made from**, so a plain document saved as a type shows the reference style among the first rows of Look afterwards.
11. **Look's foot button *Document properties…*** is gone: the top has **Document type…**. The scripts of the parts that read Look were rewritten for it, and `e2e/look.mjs` turns off *Number the lines* under Nature's look before it measures verse numbers.
12. **Editing a type writes the look and not the parts.** The brief had one function for both buttons and for editing, which would put the parts of the document into the type at the first change: a new type made from Novel lost the parts of Novel when its font was changed (found with `e2e/one-form.mjs`). Now the writing that editing does (`saveToType(…, parts = false)`) leaves the contents and the kinds as they are, and **Save to** and **Save as document type…** put the parts in. The consequence is that the parts of a type of one's own can be changed only with a change of the look beside them, since **Save to** is dimmed without one; and **Save to** puts the parts of the document in whether or not they are what the writer meant for the type (a document with only a title page makes a type of a title page and the text). The owner decided the same day that the parts of a type are edited in *Parts* while it is edited (decision 9).
13. **A look saved with a type of the writer's own is named as the type**, and the first choice of *Look taken from* then says the type's name alone (*Gentium book*), not *Gentium book, from Gentium book*. The heading *Set as “Manuscript” has them*, over *How “Text” looks…* at the foot of the kind menu, is gone: it named a look as a thing of its own and explained what the item says.
14. **The line *Editing the document type “…”* with Done stands under the head of Look**, over its parts and outside what scrolls, so that Done is in view wherever Look is opened, also at the part Document type after **New document type…** and **Edit**; the line of changes is not shown while a type is edited.
15. **`e2e/languages.mjs` goes through the new places in Norwegian**: the dialog of the new type, Look with a change made and a type being named, and the part Document type. The other scripts of the plan (`look`, `templates`, `preview`, `what`, `editors`, `scope`, `runs`, `tables`) were rewritten or only run, and `e2e/one-form.mjs` is new.
