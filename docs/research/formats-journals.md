# Journal manuscript format requirements

Research notes for Glaukopis document-format presets. Date checked for all sources: **2026-09-27**.

## How to read this file

- Every value below was taken from a page or PDF that was actually retrieved during this research. Nothing was filled in from memory. Where the source is silent the value is recorded as **not specified**.
- Three retrieval methods were used, and each source line states which one applied:
  - **live, direct**: the page or PDF was downloaded on 2026-09-27 and its full text was read.
  - **live, via WebFetch**: the publisher blocks direct downloads (HTTP 403), so the live page was read on 2026-09-27 through a fetch tool that returns extracted passages. Quotations come from that extraction.
  - **Wayback snapshot (date)**: the live page refused automated access, so the most recent Internet Archive copy was read in full. The snapshot date is given. These values may have changed since the snapshot.
- Many journals distinguish between *initial submission* (often format-free) and *accepted manuscript* (house style). Both are recorded where the source makes the distinction, because a preset needs to know which stage it serves.
- "Source says" holds a short quotation or a close paraphrase. Quotation marks indicate verbatim text.

### Note on an inconsistency in the source

The style sheet says book citations omit the publisher ("omitting the publisher and providing place and date only"), yet three of its four book examples include a publisher, for example "(New Brunswick, NJ: Rutgers University Press, 1953–55)". The essay example follows the stated rule: "(Bloomington, IN, 1995)". A preset should treat the publisher as optional until this is clarified.

**High.** The submission page was read both live and from a snapshot eleven weeks old, and the two agree. The style sheet was read in full from a snapshot six months old, at the URL the live page still links to.

### Note on differences from The Classical Quarterly

The two Cambridge classics journals disagree on almost every citation detail: author-date with bibliography (JHS) against full footnote references without bibliography (CQ); page ranges 148–49 (JHS) against 42–5 (CQ); "e.g." forbidden (JHS) against allowed in footnotes (CQ); a named Greek font (CQ) against any Unicode font (JHS).

## Observations

### What is common

A generic journal-article default that would be accepted at submission by most of the surveyed journals:

| Parameter | Common value | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Font | Times New Roman, 12 pt | Required or named as the preferred example by AHR, Past & Present, Classical Quarterly, JBL, Art Bulletin, ASR, American Anthropologist, Nature; recommended by Science. No journal forbids it |
| Line spacing | Double | Required by most journals that state a value, among them AHR, Past & Present, Classical Quarterly, Classical Philology, JBL, Art Bulletin, ASR, APSR, American Anthropologist, Nature, and PLOS ONE. Exceptions are listed below |
| Margins | 1 inch (2.54 cm) on all sides | AHR, ASR ("at least"), LSA, APA Style, AER's reference layout. JBL allows 1 to 1.25 inches. Classics journals say only "ample" |
| Page size | Rarely stated. US Letter where it is | AHR, LSA, Science. No journal names A4. European journals are silent |
| Alignment | Left, ragged right | Art Bulletin, American Anthropologist, LSA, APA Style |
| Page numbers | On | Required by AHR, Past & Present, JHS, Classical Philology, Journal of Philosophy, Art Bulletin, APSR, PLOS ONE, PNAS, The Lancet. ASR is the exception: it asks authors to remove page numbers because its system adds them |
| Anonymisation | On for humanities and social sciences | Every humanities journal that addresses the question requires it. The usual rule is third-person self-reference, not redaction |
| Abstract | 150 to 250 words | Range across the survey is 100 to 300 words |
| Keywords | 4 to 6 | Range is "at least two" to eight |
| File format | Microsoft Word (.docx) | Accepted by nearly all; see exceptions |

Other shared habits: op. cit. and loc. cit. are rejected wherever the matter is raised; "f." and "ff." are rejected in favour of inclusive page ranges by AHR, the Classical Quarterly, JHS, AJP, and Classical Philology; alt text for figures is now demanded by Oxford and Cambridge journals and by CAA.

### Where journals conflict

**Line spacing.** Mind forbids double spacing and asks for 1.15 or 1.5. The LSA style sheet asks for 1.5. AER measures length at 1.5. Science asks for single spacing. A single "manuscript" default cannot satisfy all four.

**Alignment.** The Classical Quarterly asks for justified text, footnotes included. APA Style forbids justification. LSA forbids it as well.

**First-line indentation.** APA Style requires 0.5 inch. Past & Present requires indentation. The Classical Quarterly indents all paragraphs except the first of a section. JHS asks authors to avoid automatic indentation. The Lancet forbids indents.

**Footnotes, endnotes, or none.** Footnotes: AHR, Past & Present, Classical Quarterly, JHS, Classical Philology, Mind, Critical Inquiry, JBL, History and Theory, APSR, AER. Endnotes: Art Bulletin, Language, ASR, American Anthropologist, British Journal of Sociology, AJP. Forbidden: PLOS ONE. Automated note features forbidden: The Lancet.

**Discursive notes.** Welcomed by History and Theory. Counted and accepted by PMLA and Critical Inquiry. Discouraged by AHR ("Do not use a footnote to build on your argument"), Past & Present (no "sub-texts"), and JHS. Mind asks that footnotes not be "unduly long". Capped at 100 words each by ASR. Refused by New Literary History in the 2021 copy.

**Bibliography or not.** No bibliography, full references in notes: AHR, Past & Present, Classical Quarterly, Critical Inquiry, JBL, History and Theory, Art Bulletin. Bibliography required: JHS, Classical Philology, AJP, and all author-date journals. The two Cambridge classics journals stand on opposite sides.

**Headings.** None at all: AHR (section breaks marked with #). Roman numerals: Past & Present, JHS, AER. Arabic decimal numbering: Mind, Language. Unnumbered by rule: APA. Small capitals run into the paragraph: Language. No bold at all: Classical Quarterly. Bold permitted: Art Bulletin, Mind.

**Block quotation threshold.** About 30 words (Mind); about 40 words (Language); 50 words (Past & Present, Art Bulletin); 70 words (History and Theory); more than four lines (JHS); five or more lines (JBL); "several lines" (Classical Philology). Spacing inside the block also differs: double at Past & Present, Classical Philology, and Art Bulletin; single at JBL.

**Length and what counts.** The limit runs from 8,000 words (American Anthropologist, British Journal of Sociology) to 18,000 (Language). Notes count at PMLA, Classical Quarterly, JHS, JBL, Art Bulletin, Critical Inquiry, Journal of Philosophy, Language, ASR, APSR, American Anthropologist, and the British Journal of Sociology. Notes do not count at AHR, and AHR sets a separate budget for them. The bibliography counts at the Journal of Philosophy, ASR, American Anthropologist, and the British Journal of Sociology; it does not count at PMLA, Language, and APSR. AJP, Classical Philology, AER, and PNAS state length in pages, which a word counter cannot check.

**File format.** JBL requires searchable PDF and refuses Word. Language and AER require PDF at submission. Art Bulletin and JHS refuse PDF. LaTeX sources are accepted at some stage by Mind, Language, AER, Psychological Review, Nature, and PNAS, mostly after acceptance; Science and PLOS ONE take LaTeX work only as a PDF or converted to Word. PNAS compiles with pdfLaTeX and warns against XeLaTeX and LuaLaTeX. Only Psychological Review names "Open Office format". No journal in the survey names ODT by that name.

**Quotation marks and spelling.** Single quotation marks with British spelling: Past & Present, Classical Quarterly, JHS, Mind, British Journal of Sociology. Single quotation marks with American spelling: Language. Double quotation marks by rule: History and Theory, and AJP in the 2022 copy. Double quotation marks by example only: AHR, JBL, Art Bulletin. American spelling is stated by JBL, History and Theory, Art Bulletin, and Language. The Classical Quarterly, JHS, and the British Journal of Sociology use -ize endings while keeping "analyse". History and Theory joins American spelling to British date order.

**Page ranges.** 42–5 (Classical Quarterly, Past & Present, British Journal of Sociology); 148–49 (JHS, Language); the Chicago pattern 132–33 and 101–2 (AHR examples). A citation engine needs this as a setting.

**Citation families.** Seven distinct groups appear:

1. Chicago notes without bibliography, with house variations: AHR, Critical Inquiry, History and Theory, Art Bulletin. They differ on whether the publisher appears and on whether "ibid." is allowed.
2. SBL footnote style: JBL.
3. British footnote house styles: Past & Present, Classical Quarterly. Volume numbers in lower-case roman at Past & Present.
4. Chicago author-date: APSR, AER, American Anthropologist, AJP.
5. Author-date house styles with their own punctuation: JHS, Classical Philology, Mind, Language, ASR, British Journal of Sociology.
6. APA 7 and MLA: Psychological Review, PMLA.
7. Numbered styles: Nature, Science, PNAS, PLOS ONE (Vancouver), The Lancet (Vancouver variant).

The Chicago edition named varies: 16th (AJP, stale source), 17th (AHR, Classical Philology, Critical Inquiry, JBL, APSR, CAA style guide), 18th (History and Theory, CAA preparation page).

**Greek and other scripts.** A named font is required by the Classical Quarterly (New Athena Unicode) and suggested by JBL (SBL Greek, SBL Hebrew). JHS and AJP ask for a Unicode font without prescribing one; JHS remarks that Times New Roman and Palatino Linotype work well. AJP wants the Greek font to differ from the text font. Policy on the script itself runs in opposite directions: JBL says ancient languages should normally not be transliterated; Language says everything, Greek included, should be transliterated; Classical Philology and JHS allow transliteration only for single words or short phrases; Past & Present asks for the original script followed by a translation; Art Bulletin keeps non-Roman passages out of the body text.

### Consequences for presets

- A preset must record the stage it serves. Mind, Language, APSR, AER, American Anthropologist, the British Journal of Sociology, and PNAS accept almost any format at submission and apply the house style only after acceptance. The Classical Quarterly enforces six rules before review and the rest afterwards.
- Anonymisation is a document state, not only a layout matter. Several journals require cleaned file metadata (JHS, Journal of Philosophy) and file names without the author's name (Past & Present, American Anthropologist). Two rules conflict: American Anthropologist replaces self-citations with "Author" and deletes the entries, while Mind and APSR forbid exactly that kind of placeholder or redaction.
- Line numbering is required by Mind, Nature, and PLOS ONE, and by no other journal surveyed.
- Two journals need hand verification before a preset is built: the American Journal of Philology and the British Journal of Sociology. For PMLA and Psychological Review the layout values live in the MLA Handbook and the APA Publication Manual, which were outside the reach of this survey.
