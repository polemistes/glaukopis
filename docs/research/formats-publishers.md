# Manuscript format requirements: style guides and academic publishers

Research notes for the built-in "document format" presets in Glaukopis.
All sources were checked on **2026-09-27**.

## How to read this file

- Every value in the tables comes from a page or PDF that was actually fetched and read on the date above. The third column gives a short quotation or a close paraphrase of what the source says.
- "not specified" means the source that was read does not state the parameter. It does not mean that the publisher has no rule; it may be in a document that is not public.
- These are requirements for the **manuscript the author hands in**, not for the typeset result. Where a source describes the typeset book (e.g. Springer's "Key Style Points", Cambridge's house style), this is said explicitly.
- Several official sites refuse automated requests (HTTP 403 or a bot check). In those cases the official page was read through a snapshot in the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, and the snapshot date is given. A snapshot is a copy of the official page, but it may be older than the live page, so confidence is lowered accordingly.
- Translations from Norwegian and paraphrases are mine; quotations in quotation marks are verbatim.

## Observations

### What is common (a sensible generic default)

1. **Publishers ask for structure, not appearance.** Almost every book publisher says that the layout will be replaced in typesetting and that formatting should be kept minimal (University of Chicago Press, Princeton, Yale, Routledge, Brill, Springer Nature, Universitetsforlaget, Cappelen Damm, MHRA, De Gruyter's 2012 sheet). Several say outright that font and spacing do not matter (Universitetsforlaget, Cappelen Damm, Routledge's sample chapter). What they do require is that headings, block quotations, notes and callouts can be identified unambiguously, preferably through Word's built-in styles (Heading 1–3, the footnote and endnote function, the table function).
2. **Microsoft Word (.docx) is the universal exchange format.** Every publisher that names a format names Word. LaTeX is accepted by Springer Nature, Elsevier, OUP journals, some T&F and SAGE journals, and by agreement or under separate guidelines at the University of Chicago Press, Princeton and (for print-ready files) OUP; SAGE books accept only Word. Brill and Yale also accept RTF. **No source that was read mentions ODT**; De Gruyter's 2012 sheet mentions "Open Office" as a word processor. Brill and Yale want a matching PDF alongside the Word file; Cambridge, Routledge and Princeton want one when special characters or mathematics occur.
3. **Where a layout is prescribed, it clusters tightly:** 12 pt Times New Roman or a similar serif face (APA, Turabian, MLA, T&F journals, SAGE books, Universitetsforlaget's open access guide, MHRA without a size); margins of 1 in. or 2.5 cm (Turabian, MLA, APA, SBL, T&F journals, Bloomsbury, one Cappelen Damm series); double line spacing (APA, MLA, Turabian body text, SBL body text, T&F journals, SAGE books, Bloomsbury, Chicago's manuscript rule) with 1.5 as the accepted alternative (MHRA, Universitetsforlaget open access, Libelli).
4. **Left-aligned with a ragged right margin** is required by APA, MLA, Turabian, SBL, Elsevier and Universitetsforlaget. **No hyphenation at line ends** is required by APA, MLA, Elsevier, Routledge, Universitetsforlaget and De Gruyter's 2012 sheet. Only one source asks for justified text (the Cappelen Damm series "Kunst og kultur i opplæring").
5. **Notes are made with the word processor's note function** (University of Chicago Press, Princeton, Yale, Routledge, De Gruyter's 2012 sheet; Bloomsbury accepts it) **and are numbered from 1 in each chapter** (Princeton, Yale, Routledge, Brill, Cambridge, Bloomsbury). No note markers on headings or titles (MHRA, Brill, Cambridge, Princeton, Yale).
6. **Heading hierarchy must be strict, with no skipped levels** (Cambridge, Springer Nature, SAGE). Three levels is the usual ceiling for books (Routledge: three levels of subheading; Cappelen Damm: three levels within a chapter; Universitetsforlaget open access: three levels, counting the chapter title as the first; Bloomsbury names three categories). SAGE books allow four; Brill wants more than four avoided; APA, T&F journals and SBL define five. No final punctuation after a heading (MLA, Turabian, MHRA, Cambridge, Springer Nature, De Gruyter's 2012 sheet).
7. **Block quotations are indented on the left and have no quotation marks.** This is universal.
8. **Reference style is increasingly free, provided it is consistent.** Routledge, Brill, Cambridge, Princeton, Universitetsforlaget, Elsevier, Wiley and OUP journals all accept any consistent system, at least at submission. Chicago is the most frequently preferred style for humanities books (University of Chicago Press, Princeton, Yale, Brill, Bloomsbury's short-title system, Libelli, Edda); APA is the preferred style in the social sciences (Brill), at SAGE books, and as the fallback at both Norwegian publishers (Cappelen Damm names APA 7 specifically).
9. **Reference lists per chapter** are required or preferred wherever digital chapter sales matter (Routledge, Springer Nature, Brill, Universitetsforlaget open access). Princeton is the exception and wants one consolidated bibliography.
10. **Chapter abstracts and keywords are now normal for books**, for online discovery: Routledge (100–200 words, mandatory), Springer Nature (at most 200 words), Brill (about 200 words, 6–10 keywords), Universitetsforlaget open access (150–250 words, 4–5 keywords), Cappelen Damm series (150–250 words). Journal keyword counts cluster at 3–6.
11. **Alt text for images** is now required by many publishers (University of Chicago Press, Princeton, Yale, Cambridge, De Gruyter, Universitetsforlaget, Cappelen Damm, Springer Nature recommends it). A format preset may need a place for it.
12. **Anonymisation** is a journal-level matter (Cambridge, SAGE, Edda), handled by moving identifying material to a separate title page file. Among book publishers only Brill and the Libelli series ask for an anonymised manuscript.

A generic default that would offend none of the sources: A4 or US Letter with 2.5 cm (1 in.) margins, a 12 pt serif Unicode font, double or 1.5 line spacing, left-aligned with hyphenation off, up to three unnumbered heading levels distinguished by Word heading styles (bold, bold italic, italic), block quotations indented 1.27 cm (0.5 in.) without quotation marks, automatically numbered notes restarting per chapter, page numbers present.

### Surprises and things to be aware of

- **The Chicago Manual is in its 18th edition (2024), but the official free paper-format tip sheets are still those of 2018**, tied to Turabian 9th ed. and CMOS 17th ed. Yale already cites the 18th edition; Princeton cites the 16th or 17th; Bloomsbury's house style cites the 16th; Springer Nature's humanities reference style is "based on" the 15th.
- **Harvard University Press publishes no manuscript guidelines at all**; they are given to authors privately. De Gruyter likewise keeps its style sheets with the editors.
- **MLA 9 no longer says "12 points"**: it allows "anywhere between 11 and 13 points".
- **APA 7 allows several fonts**, including sans serif faces and 12 pt Aptos (the font page was updated in February 2026), so an APA preset should not hard-code Times New Roman.
- **Yale's block quotation threshold is 100 words**, more than double the usual figure.
- **Springer Nature contradicts itself on keywords**: three to six on the web page, 5–10 in the April 2025 PDF. The PDFs linked from Springer's live page sit on a host (`dam.springernature.com`) that did not resolve on the day of checking.
- **Universitetsforlaget's two book guides differ** (layout irrelevant and blank lines between paragraphs, against Times New Roman 12 pt with 1.5 spacing), and **Cappelen Damm's two series guides contradict each other** on almost every typographic value. Presets for the Norwegian publishers should therefore be labelled by guide or series, not by publisher alone.
- **Several major publishers block automated reading of their author guidelines** (OUP, Cambridge's Author Hub, MLA Handbook Plus, APA Style, Bloomsbury, SAGE's PDF downloads, scup.com and idunn.no). OUP is the weakest entry in this file for that reason and should be checked by hand.
- **Journals at the large commercial publishers have, in practice, no publisher-wide format.** Elsevier ("Your Paper Your Way"), Wiley ("Free Format"), Taylor & Francis ("format-free submission"), OUP and Springer Nature all defer to the individual journal or accept any consistent layout at first submission. Taylor & Francis is the only one that still publishes a concrete default layout (Times New Roman 12 pt, double spacing, margins of at least 2.5 cm, five heading levels). A publisher-level journal preset is therefore only meaningful for Taylor & Francis; for the others the preset would have to be per journal.
- **ODT is not mentioned as an accepted format by any source read.**
