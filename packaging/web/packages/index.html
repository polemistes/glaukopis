What Glaukopis is

Glaukopis is a writing program for texts that begin as ideas: a book, an essay, a thesis, a report, a novel, a play, a course of lectures. You do not start from a blank page. You lay out what you think as a map of ideas, each with a name and, when you are ready, its text. The map is the outline of the text, and the text is the map written out, so there is no moment when the plan is put away and the writing begins. A thought that has no place yet keeps until it finds one, and any branch of the map, a chapter or a single idea, can be read and printed as a text of its own.

Beside your projects stands your library: what you have read, with your own notes on each work, cited in the text with a keystroke and set in the style a publisher asks for. The finished text comes out as a PDF, a Word or LibreOffice document, LaTeX, a web page or an e-book, with its notes, figures, tables and bibliography in place. Books and papers you have as PDFs or scans can be read into a project. The interface speaks eight languages, and spelling is checked in more. Two or more people can write in the same project at the same time, through a server of your own. Everything stays on your computer and nothing is sent anywhere; the program is free software, for Linux, Windows and macOS.

New in 0.2.3

  • Reading PDFs and scans, made over. The window opens as a short summary: what the file is, whether its pages take the text they have or are read anew (or each by the quality of its text), and how Tesseract reads them. Look at the pages and try them shows every page, the text a page has beside what Tesseract reads of it, and how sure Tesseract was (low, good or high, in percent), with the words it doubted underlined. Settings can differ from page to page, each trial is kept to compare, and whether a PDF becomes a tree by its headings or by its pages is chosen after it is read.
  • Books in Gothic type. Two new languages for reading scans: Tesseract's Fraktur model, and Fraktur, Danish and Norwegian, taught æ, ø and å for books printed in Norway and Denmark before about 1900. The long s (ſ) is read and kept as s.
  • What you are writing is chosen from one button over the preview, and the Look panel follows the page from top to bottom. About this document holds a document's details and its language.
  • The title page, the abstract and the contents stand where the template puts them, in every format: PDF, Word, Writer, LaTeX, the web page and the e-book.
  • Every line of the preview says what it is. Pressing a line shows a chip naming it, a quotation, a heading, the running head, a page number, and the chip opens Look at it.
  • Headers and footers hold what you put in them, left, centre and right, on the first page and the rest; headings are numbered level by level; and an element with a look of its own keeps it wherever it is printed.
  • Every window can be moved by dragging its title; a double-click on the title puts it back in the middle.
  • The interface is complete in eight languages again: Danish, German, English, French, Italian, Norwegian Bokmål and Nynorsk, and Swedish, with every word of what is new.
  • Faster and steadier. Long texts take typing without delay, large BibTeX files and libraries with many files open at once, and a review of the code fixed some two hundred faults.

What was new in the versions before: the notes of every release.

Not signed. The installers for Windows and macOS carry no signature, so both systems warn before they run. On Windows, choose More info and then Run anyway. On macOS, open Glaukopis the first time by right-clicking it in Applications and choosing Open; if macOS says the app is damaged, run xattr -cr /Applications/Glaukopis.app in the Terminal and try again.

Spelling and other languages. No dictionaries of spelling come with Glaukopis, and Tesseract, which reads text in scans and pictures, comes with English only. The languages you need are imported in Glaukopis itself, under Settings › Language: choose Import spelling dictionaries or Import OCR language files, and Import beside a language. They are fetched from this page.

Languages of spelling and of reading text

Glaukopis imports these itself, from the list index.json, and checks each against the checksum it gives. They are here as well for a computer without the network, or for a server of your own: download them with index.json into one folder, and give that folder as the server with Change beside the server's address in the same places in the settings. How such a package is made is told in docs/language-packages.md.

Checking what you downloaded

SHA256SUMS lists the checksum of every installer and package above. In the folder you downloaded to, sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing on Linux or shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing on macOS checks them; on Windows, Get-FileHash in PowerShell shows a file's checksum to compare. The checksums of the languages are in index.json.

Reading on, and reporting

The guide to Glaukopis is installed with it and can be read on GitHub: docs/guide.md. Faults, surprises and wishes are welcome by mail to develop@robertemilberge.no, or as issues at github.com/polemistes/glaukopis. Say which file you installed and what you did when it went wrong. The interface speaks many languages, most of them translated with the help of a language model and not yet read by people who speak them: a word that is wrong in yours is worth a line.
